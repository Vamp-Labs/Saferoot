SafeRoot Policy

One Safe approval on Ethereum. Trustless verification via Creditcoin's Attestcoin oracle. Execution that refuses anything that wasn't exactly what was approved. No bridge, no new signers.

01 — The Problem

Cross-chain treasury authority means trusting something new

Safe multisig is the standard for DAO and protocol treasuries — but its authority stops at the chain it lives on. The moment a team wants an approved action to affect another chain, every common answer introduces a new party the treasury has to trust.

Bridge the assets

A bridge contract or its operators become a single point of failure holding the funds. Bridges are, by dollar value, the most exploited category of smart contract in crypto history.

Second signer set

Deploying a separate multisig on the destination chain doubles key-management surface and governance overhead — now two committees must stay honest and in sync.

Centralized relayer

A single company or server sits between a governance decision and the funds moving — exactly the kind of intermediary decentralized treasuries exist to avoid.

None of these actually need to exist. A DAO doesn't need to move assets cross-chain to act cross-chain — it needs the destination chain to be convinced, cryptographically, that an approval genuinely happened. That's a proof problem, not a bridging problem.

02 — The Solution

Approve once. Prove it. Verify it natively. Enforce it exactly.

STEP 1

Approve on Ethereum

A Safe calls PolicyRegistry.registerPolicy() with an explicit, bounded policy: target contract, function, exact calldata, value cap, time window, expiry. An ordinary Safe tx — no module, no new signer installed.

→
STEP 2

Attestcoin proves it

Creditcoin's decentralized oracle builds attestor consensus over Ethereum's history and produces a cryptographic inclusion proof for that exact transaction.

→
STEP 3

Verify natively

SafeRootPolicyExecutor hands the proof to Creditcoin's native 0xFD2 Query Verifier precompile — a chain-level primitive, not a third-party oracle contract.

→
STEP 4

Execute under constraints

Anyone can call executeAction() — the contract re-derives and checks every parameter against what the Safe approved. Any deviation reverts with a named error.

The result: a DAO's Ethereum Safe becomes the source of authority for actions on Creditcoin, with zero new trusted parties in between.

04 — Why This Fits Creditcoin

A real integration of Attestcoin, not a mock of the interface

  • Attestcoin / USC is Creditcoin's answer to trustless cross-chain data — SafeRoot integrates it directly (@gluwa/usc-sdk on the backend, @gluwa/asc-contracts proof types on-chain), not a stand-in interface.
  • The native 0xFD2 Query Verifier precompile is called directly from AttestcoinVerifierAdapter.sol — proof verification happens at the chain level, exactly the trust-minimization story Creditcoin's stack is built to enable.
  • Building this surfaced a real integration trap worth documenting: Attestcoin's internal chainKey for a source chain is not the same as its EVM chainId. Sepolia's EVM chainId is 11155111; its Attestcoin chainKey is 1.
We hit this as a real production bug: a misconfigured adapter caused every proof verification to revert with UnsupportedSourceChain until we root-caused it by decoding the raw revert data byte-by-byte. It's documented here because it's exactly the trap a team using Attestcoin for the first time will hit.

08 — What We'd Build Next

Beyond the hackathon

  • Guardian rotation & multi-guardian quorum — today a single guardian address can pause; a natural extension is a small guardian committee with its own threshold.
  • Policy templates as a public registry — let any Creditcoin protocol publish an "integration" (target + allowed selectors) so more DAOs can point a policy at it without custom backend wiring.
  • Batch policies — one Safe approval authorizing a sequence of dependent actions across multiple Creditcoin contracts, not just parallel independent actions.
  • Mainnet hardening — formal audit of SafeRootPolicyExecutor's replay/expiry/reentrancy boundaries before any real-value mainnet deployment.
